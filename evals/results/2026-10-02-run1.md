### ✅ Jargon · What is NAV?
Tools: explain_term · Model: groq

> **NAV (Net Asset Value)** – the price of one mutual‑fund unit. It’s calculated daily by adding up the fund’s assets, subtracting liabilities, and dividing by the total units outstanding.
> 
> - Example: If NAV = ₹50 and you invest ₹5,000, you receive 100 units (₹5,000 ÷ ₹50).  
> - NAV changes every working day as the market value of the fund’s holdings moves.  
> 
> So, NAV tells you how much one unit of the fund is worth at that moment.

### ✅ Jargon · What is an expense ratio?
Tools: explain_term · Model: groq

> **Expense ratio** – the yearly fee a mutual‑fund house charges to run the fund.  
> It’s taken as a small % of the amount you have invested.  
> 
> * Example: a 1 % expense ratio on ₹1,00,000 costs about ₹1,000 a year.  
> * It’s deducted before you see any returns, so a lower ratio usually leaves you more money.  
> 
> (Expense ratio is not a guarantee of performance; it’s just a cost.)

### ✅ Jargon · Direct vs regular plan, which is better?
Tools: explain_term · Model: groq

> Direct plan usually gives you a lower expense ratio because it skips the distributor’s commission.  
> 
> - **Cost** – Direct plans charge less, so more of your money stays invested.  
> - **Control** – You pick the fund yourself; you won’t get advice from a broker.  
> - **When to use** – If you’re comfortable researching funds, a direct plan is typically better. If you prefer professional help, a regular plan may suit you, but the higher fees will reduce long‑term growth.  
> 
> (Expense ratio differences can add up over many years.)

### ✅ Jargon · What does exit load mean?
Tools: explain_term · Model: groq

> **Exit load** – a small fee a mutual fund may charge if you redeem (sell) your units before a set holding period, usually 1 year.  
> 
> - It’s a percentage of the amount you withdraw (e.g., 1 % on ₹10,000 = ₹100).  
> - The fee discourages short‑term trading and covers transaction costs.  
> - If you stay invested beyond the lock‑in period, no exit load is applied.  
> 
> (Always check a fund’s scheme document for the exact rate and period.)

### ✅ Jargon · What is an index fund?
Tools: explain_term · Model: groq

> **Index fund** – A fund that simply copies a market index, like the Nifty 50, instead of a manager picking stocks.  
> 
> - Holds the same stocks (and in the same weight) as the chosen index.  
> - Usually has lower expense ratios because there’s no active management.  
> - Returns move almost exactly with the index’s performance, so they can go up **or** down.  
> 
> (Investing in an index fund still carries market risk; past returns don’t guarantee future results.)
